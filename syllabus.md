## Course Components

### Lecture
Lecture slides and assignments will be linked on the [calendar page](../calendar).

### Assignments
Exact due dates for assignments will be shown in Gradescope, but in general:

- Homework and worksheets will be released after lecture on Mondays
- Homeworks (HW) will be due 2 weeks after release (unless stated otherwise) at 11:59 pm
- Worksheets (WS) will be due the following Monday at 11:59 pm

### Ed Discussion
We will primarily use Ed for discussion outside of lecture and office hours. The system is highly catered to getting you help fast and efficiently from classmates and the teaching staff. Rather than emailing, we encourage you to post your questions on Ed. If it is something serious or you are having difficulty with the course, then please reach out directly to the instructor over email (afuji@engineering.upenn.edu).

## Communication & Getting Help

### Getting Help
Due to our smaller class size, I'm in a position that enables me to provide more personalized support.  I'm very open to scheduling one-on-one meetings either on Zoom or in my office (Levine 269C).  If you wish to do so, please email me at afuji@engineering.upenn.edu.  You may request any of the following:

- help with catching up on lecture or understanding lecture material
- going over previous in-class problems together
- help with catching up on past homework
- advice about Penn/student life
- anything else you may want! This is not an exhaustive list

If your situation doesn't quite match the above scenario but you still wish to contact me or Foram, you are always welcome to do so.

### Inclusion
You have a right to a learning environment that feels safe, where you are treated with respect and feel that you can contribute ideas constructively. Put another way, you have the right to an inclusive learning environment.

We want every student to feel that they have a safe and healthy learning environment, and if at any point you feel like this is not the case in any way, please let course staff know and we will see what we can do to help.

## Policies

### Grading

The grading breakdown is as follows:

- Six homeworks (70%)
- Completion-graded worksheets (20%)
- Participation (10%)

One way participation is graded is by the Ed exercises done in class (they will auto-lock ~30 min after the class period ends).  The bar for what counts as good participation fairly low, and you will hit it as long as you attempt the exercises in good faith.  It's ok if you're not able to get through all of them in the time allotted - we're not factoring that into our grading.

Other ways to get participation credit:
- OH attendance (one-on-one meetings fall under OH attendance if you cannot make the provided times)
- Ed discussion (either answering or asking questions)

### Resubmission Policy
Instead of a late day policy, we will give you the chance to request a previous assignment to be reopened.  This will be done on a Google Form released every other Monday, specifically the odd-numbered weeks.  Forms will generally be released before lecture and close at 11:59 pm the same day.  Only one assignment may be reopened on Gradescope at a time, and remains open for either one week (for worksheets) or two weeks (homeworks).

The intention surrounding this policy is firstly to let you act on any improvements you've made within the semester that came after an assignment's due date.  Every student learns at a different speed, and my hope is that this policy reduces the "penalty" felt when it takes longer to fully grasp and leverage a concept.  If you're interested, [you can learn more about mastery grading here](https://teaching.unl.edu/resources/alternative-grading/mastery-grading/).  The second use case of a resubmission policy is to assist the process of catching up after falling behind.  As a student myself, I've fallen behind in class for various reasons, and I don't judge students who find themselves needing to catch up.  If you believe extra support would be helpful during a catchup period, please refer to the section above: [Getting Help](#getting-help).

Resubmissions apply to both worksheets and homeworks, but are best used on homework assignments because the worksheets are completion-based and don't receive feedback us.  Due to there not being a late-day policy, Gradescope submissions are locked at the exact due time.  Any assignments that are missing a submission by this time will be graded a 0 (though you can always improve that score by reopening it).

If there is something that comes up that prevents you from participating in class or completing assignments, please contact the instructor.  I don't need to know all of the details (you have a right to privacy), and I will work with you to find a suitable arrangement.

### Collaboration
You are permitted (and encouraged) to discuss the homework problems with other class members, but these discussions are to be limited to high-level concepts. In particular, you are absolutely not permitted to copy/share code or implementation details. Similarly, you are not permitted to use or consult code found on the internet for any of your assignments.

Similarly, you are not allowed to upload your code to publicly accessible places (like public GitHub repositories), and you are not allowed to access anyone else’s code. If you discover someone else’s code online, please report it to the course staff via a private note on Ed.

### Generative AI

We recognize the increasing prevalence of generative AI tools in a variety of applications. We acknowledge that AI can be a useful learning tool. It can help clarify ideas or help you explore related concepts. However, it can also [generate plenty of authoritative-sounding nonsense information](https://www.aisnakeoil.com/p/chatgpt-is-a-bullshit-generator-but). Without some understanding of fundamental concepts and knowing how to cross-reference model output against an external non-generative-model source of information, it can be hard to tell the difference. Our goal is to help you develop a strong understanding of fundamental concepts and processes without relying on generative AI.

I understand it's also tempting to reach to reach to GenAI when you feel short on time or feel particularly stuck.  I intend for the resubmission policy to ease some of the time-related anxieties, and I wish to work with you if there is a concern that this policy is unable to address.  Feeling stuck is also an expected and beneficial part of learning.  If you're here to learn to program in Python (which I hope that is the case), relying on external tools [robs you of the actual learning experience](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6868618) and will work against your goal.  For the most accurate content, I highly recommend reading the [official Python documentation](https://docs.python.org/3/), and asking questions to your fellow classmates, your TA, or myself.

To this end:
- Avoid using AI to generate content summaries.
- It is forbidden to use AI to generate code or text for assignments (take-home or in-class).
- Do not present AI-generated content as your own work or without citation.

If you have questions or concerns, feel free to reach out.
